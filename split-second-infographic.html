Demonstrated · technically proven

What already works

Four independent ways to put a name to a photo — all validated.

GPX ↔ Photo

Location + time match

With good data, a GPX track matches a photo taken near where the athlete was at that instant.

Face

Facial recognition

The user uploads a selfie; we find their other photos. Great when the face is visible.

Body

Physical-dimension match

When the surfer faces away, we line up their body proportions against a known anchor photo.

Δt

Time-drift calibration

The user films the camera's on-screen clock; we compute the offset between camera time and watch time.

The long tail · where it gets hard

Where complexity creeps in

None of these are dealbreakers — but each one is effort spent on the last few percent.

Distant cameraman

Matching degrades when the photographer is far from the surfer.

No GPS on the camera

Even modern DSLRs often lack coordinates and need an add-on module.

Clock drift

Camera time slips from watch time — from normal drift or write-to-disk lag.

Track upload needed

GPS matching only fires if the user hands over their track.

The realisation

An 80 / 20 problem

~80% SOLVED WITH PROVEN TECH — SHIP THIS
~20% LONG TAIL — LATER

The 80% is already handled. Grinding on the final 5–10% is where all the complexity lives — for what is, at the end of the day, one job: match a photo to a surfer. Solve the bulk now; the long tail is a small fraction of users.

The strategy · set the bar

Minimum requirements

Meet these three and the proven tech covers a huge share of photos.

1

Geotag on upload

Uploaded photos must carry geolocation data.

2

Clocks set right

Camera time is configured correctly before shooting.

3

Selfie or track

To find their shots, users upload a selfie or a GPX track.

Next step

Turn what we have into a demonstrable product running on canned data — ready to put in front of a development partner. Prove the 80% first; chase the long tail once there's a partner on board.
